A FARM WITH A LITTLE MATH

What makes a good guard?

Every run compares zero, one and two alpacas over 10 trials. The Java engine samples predator losses and guard maintenance, then adds them up.

Animal losses + guard fees + maintenance
= total model cost

One alpaca halves the modelled risk; two reduce it to a quarter. Sheep are valued at $150, lambs at $250 and alpacas at $1,000. Each guard adds a $500 fixed fee and $400–$600 maintenance (halved if it dies).

The seed makes the comparison repeatable. The lowest sample mean wins; a tie favours fewer guards. Ten trials cannot establish a reliable real-world saving.

The animation replays each trial’s actual loss counts. Positions, wandering and timing are decorative; trials are independent scenarios, not consecutive days. An alpaca’s death does not change protection within this simplified model.
